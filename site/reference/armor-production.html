Context

Armor production divides cleanly into two arguments the chart makes visible at a glance: how many, and how good. The Soviet and American bands answer the first. The T-34 family crosses seventy thousand hulls and the M4 Sherman nearly fifty thousand. Single types out-built the entire armored output of some nations, and their ribbons run so wide they intentionally exceed the chart's nominal ceiling.

Germany's band answers the second, and at a cost. The most-produced German armored vehicle was not a tank at all but the StuG III assault gun, cheaper and faster to build on the proven Panzer III chassis. The Panther and the two Tigers carry famous names and thin ribbons: the Tiger II totals under five hundred, a rounding error against Soviet and American volume. German engineering chased overmatch on the individual vehicle while losing the production war decisively.

Britain's contribution is the long middle (Valentine, Churchill, Cromwell), steady infantry and cruiser tanks, with the Valentine widely shipped to the USSR under Lend-Lease. Japan's four types barely register: armor was a low priority against naval steel, and the late Chi-He and Chi-Nu, the first Japanese tanks that could trade blows with Allied mediums, arrived in the low hundreds.

The post-war tails matter for the bench. The T-34/85, M26 Pershing, and others kept rolling past 1945; the dashed sections separate the wartime vehicle a modeler is usually after from the Cold-War continuation that shares its name but not its markings.

Sources & method

  • Method: ribbon half-width = MAX_HALF · √(units that year / MAX_RATE), the same square-root rate scale as the aircraft chart. The Sherman and T-34 peaks slightly exceed the scale ceiling by design. Their output dwarfed every other type. Totals are standard figures; annual splits are approximate.
  • Zaloga, Steven J. Osprey New Vanguard series and related titles. (T-34, Sherman, and Soviet/US production figures and yearly output.)
  • Jentz, Thomas L. Panzer Tracts series. (German AFV production records by year and variant.)
  • Chamberlain, Peter, and Chris Ellis. British and American Tanks of World War II. (British and US type totals.)
  • Standard national production records as compiled in the type monographs above. (Year-by-year output.)
